# Technical criticism and falsification

We welcome negative results, competing explanations, replications, and narrowly stated corrections. Cite the paper version and claim or section being challenged. Explain your method, assumptions, observations, expected result, actual result, and what would change your conclusion. Distinguish a conceptual objection, implementation observation, empirical replication, and new hypothesis.

For a proposed experiment, freeze the task design, scoring, failure handling, budgets, and comparison conditions before measuring. Include failed and incomplete trials. Identify repeated designs rather than treating repeated calls as independent samples. Keep original results alongside separately versioned corrections; do not retrospectively rescore a published experiment without explicitly identifying a new analysis.

Use wholly synthetic, disclosure-safe examples. Do not upload personal records, credentials, private source, private logs, or operational access details. The v0.1 challenge has a behavioral contract and an owner-operated local sandbox. No internet endpoint has been announced. Test only your own sandbox or an explicitly designated challenge endpoint; private and production systems are excluded. Use the failure-report template and omit session credentials.

GitHub issues may contain public technical criticism and public-safe reproduction notes. Pull requests may propose corrections to this documentation. A contribution is not evidence of author endorsement or peer-review acceptance. Contributions to original documentation, fixtures, and client examples are offered under CC BY 4.0; you must have the rights to submit them. No contribution requires disclosing proprietary mechanisms.
